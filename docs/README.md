# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

Welcome to the OctoAcme project management documentation. This folder contains standardized guidance for running cross-functional projects from initiation through release and continuous improvement.

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a structured but flexible approach centered on customer value, iterative delivery, clear ownership, and data-informed decisions. The process documents define the roles, artifacts, workflows, communication practices, quality controls, and decision points that help teams deliver transparently and consistently.

## The OctoAcme Project Lifecycle

1. **Initiation** — Validate the business need, align stakeholders, define success metrics, and make a go/no-go decision.
2. **Planning** — Break the initiative into shippable increments, prioritize and estimate the backlog, and identify milestones, risks, and dependencies.
3. **Execution** — Build, test, review, and track work through the project board while managing blockers and communicating progress.
4. **Release** — Deploy with acceptance, CI, security, smoke-test, rollback, and post-deployment verification checks.
5. **Retrospective** — Capture learnings, assign improvement actions, and measure their impact after sprints, releases, milestones, or incidents.

## Core Practices at a Glance

- **Ownership:** Project Managers coordinate delivery, Product Managers own outcomes and prioritization, and Developers implement and test solutions. QA/Testing and stakeholders contribute validation, input, and approvals.
- **Communication:** Teams use standups, weekly delivery or PM syncs, demos, milestone updates, and stakeholder communications to maintain alignment. Risks and blockers follow a team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor escalation path.
- **Quality:** Pull requests should be small where possible, include issue links and acceptance criteria, pass automated tests and linting, and receive the required review approval. Unit, integration, end-to-end smoke, security, and manual acceptance testing are applied as appropriate.
- **Transparency:** Teams maintain project boards, one-pagers, release plans, risk registers, status updates, and retrospective actions as shared sources of truth.
